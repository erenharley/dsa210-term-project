## Project goal

Analyze how political, regional, and global shocks affect tourism demand in Turkey across 15 source markets (2003–2025). The proposal commits to three deliverables:

1. **Market sensitivity** — which source markets over/underreact to which shock type
2. **Recovery rates** — how fast each market bounces back, and whether speed differs by shock or geography
3. **Forward-looking targeting** — actionable recommendations for Turkish tourism firms in the context of **rising MENA tensions (2023+)**

Goal #3 is the headline of the proposal and must be addressed by at least one hypothesis test. (See H6 below — added in the Phase 3 redesign.)

## Hypothesis tests (Phase 3, post-redesign)

| # | Claim | Test | Pairs with figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Market groups respond differently to Turkey's 2016 coup | One-way ANOVA across groups + **Tukey HSD post-hoc** | Fig 1 |
| H2 | MENA-origin markets fell harder than non-MENA during the Syrian war (2011–15) | Welch t-test | Fig 2 |
| H3 | Recovery speed after COVID differs by group | One-way ANOVA on years-to-recover + **Tukey HSD post-hoc** | Fig 3 |
| H4 | Turkey lira weakness correlates with **larger** visitor drops during shocks (crisis effect dominates the cheap-Turkey price effect) | Pearson correlation | Fig 4 |
| H5 | Source country GDP per capita predicts visitor volume | Pearson + Spearman correlation | Fig 5 |
| H6 | **Post-2023 regional tensions reduced MENA-origin tourism more than non-MENA** | **Welch t-test: 2023–2025 mean vs 2017–2019 baseline, MENA vs non-MENA** | **Fig 6 (NEW)** |

**Why Tukey HSD on H1 and H3:** ANOVA is omnibus — it only tells you "at least one group differs." Pairwise directional claims like "Western Europe is most sensitive" or "Former Soviet is slowest to recover" require post-hoc pairwise comparisons with controlled family-wise error. Without Tukey HSD, those README claims are technically overclaims.

## Figure design philosophy (post-redesign)

**Every figure must:**

1. Have a **finding-driven title** — a sentence stating what the figure proves, not a description of what's plotted. ❌ "Visitor trends 2003–2025" ✓ "Western Europe dropped 3× harder than MENA during Turkey's 2016 coup."
2. Be **visually tied to a single hypothesis test**. The reader should be able to see the test result in the chart, not just the data.
3. Include the **test statistic and p-value** as on-figure annotation (small box, not buried in caption).
4. **Not bend visuals or titles to a desired story.** If a Tukey HSD comes back saying Western Europe is *not* significantly more sensitive than Eastern Europe at α=0.05, the title and the README finding both get softened. Don't fake it. This is non-negotiable — a sharp grader will catch overclaims.

**Style:** light grid, white background, one accent color per market group (consistent across all figures), legible at print size.

## Figure spec (post-redesign — 6 figures, 1:1 with hypotheses)

| Fig | Hypothesis | Visual | Key annotation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | H1 — coup sensitivity by group | Strip/dot plot: one dot per country, x = % drop in 2016, y = market group; group means as horizontal lines | ANOVA F, p; Tukey pairs |
| 2 | H2 — MENA vs non-MENA, Syria | Slope chart: each country's mean visitors 2008–10 → 2011–15, lines colored MENA vs non-MENA; small inset showing t-test distributions | Welch t, p; mean change per group |
| 3 | H3 — COVID recovery curves | Small multiples per market group: mean (visitors / 2018-19 baseline) trajectory 2019–2025, horizontal line at 100%, star at year of crossover | ANOVA F, p; Tukey pairs; "not recovered" list |
| 4 | H4 — lira weakness vs drops | Scatter: x = Turkey PPP rate (or YoY % change), y = % visitor drop during shock years; one dot per country-shock; OLS line; colored by group | Pearson r, p; "Naïve theory predicted positive slope. Observed: negative." |
| 5 | H5 — source GDP vs visitors | Scatter: x = log(source GDP per capita), y = log(visitors); LOWESS or OLS line; colored by group | Pearson r, Spearman r, p; "weak but significant" framing |
| 6 | **H6 — post-2023 MENA tensions** | Bar chart of `(2023–25 mean / 2017–19 baseline − 1) × 100` per country, MENA highlighted in accent color; or small-multiples of post-2023 trajectory specifically for MENA vs non-MENA | Welch t, p |

**Russia-TUR 2015 dropped from Fig 2 quadrants** — it's a one-country (Russia) one-year event mostly driven by Russia's tourism ban. Not a market-group-level phenomenon. Doesn't earn a figure quadrant. Mention in narrative only if useful.
